Title: Bayesian Local Null Testing and Variable Selection in Semi-parametric models
Abstract: 

It is often of interest to know not only whether something has an effect, but also when and where the effect is present.  Testing of local effects, however, poses major methodological difficulties, particularly when using expressive models able to capture non-linear relationships.  In the first portion of this talk, we adopt the additive model framework, in which non-linear function spaces are linearized via basis expansion, and develop the concept of an orthogonal cut basis which yields consistent local null tests.  We formalize local null testing as Bayesian model selection with an orthogonal cut basis, applying the method to brain imaging data in order to describe neural responses to stimuli.  The second portion of this talk discusses ongoing work in applying Bayesian variable selection methods to deep neural networks, increasing parsimony and interpretability while preserving the expressivity afforded by deep neural networks.

  - BUT LOCAL NULL TESTING HAS PROBLEMS
    - just kept getting weird results.  Performed nominally well at low n, but poorly at very high n, or when taking error terms out of simulated data.
    - we like to be able to SHARE information across observations.  BUT sharing information in this context is problematic.  Instead of "SHARING", estimation of an effect at active coordinates CONTAMINATES estimation of effect at null coordinates.

  - MISSPECIFIED --> ORTHOGONAL CUT BASIS
      - strikes balance between sharing information but also LOCALIZING information.
      - for any choice of knots, prevents false positives
      - can handle independent as well as correlated data
      - can be extended to multivariate $z_i$
      - orthogonal cut basis is a specific projection of the deisgn matrix.  Standard computational and inferential machinery is still available / applicable.

  - The major advantages of using Bayesian machinery stem from being able to put a prior on models.
       1. takes care of the multiple testing problem (Mueller reference)
       1. allows multi-resolution analysis
       1. even allows smoothing of estimates via Bayesian Model Averaging
    
  - Method
    - Truth:
      $$E_{\mathcal{F}}(y_i | z_i, x_i) = f_0(z_i) + f_1(x_i, z_i)$$
      - f_0 is baseline, x_i is vector of covariates
      - sum-to-zero identifiability condition on $f_1$, i.e. $f_1$ is the deviation from the baseline
    - local null hypothesis for covariate $j$ at $z_i$: does $f_1(x_i, z_i)$ depend on $x_{ij}$?

    - Model:
      $E_\eta (y_i | z_i, x_i) = \beta_0 (z_i) + \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_{1j}(z_i)x_{ij} = w_{i0}^T (z_i) \eta_0 + \sum_{j=1}^p  w_{ij}^T (z_i)\eta_{1j} x_{ij}$
      - varying coefficient model (citation - Tibshirani?)
      - $w_{i0}^T (z_i)$ is a basis for $\beta_0 (z_i)$
      - $w_{ij}^T (z_i)$ is a basis for $\beta_{1j} (z_i)$
      Model-based local null: if $w_{ij}^T (z_i)\eta_{1j} = 0$, then $\beta_{1j}(z_i) = 0$, i.e. no effect of $x_{ij}$ on $y_i$ at $z_i$
  
  - if W_1 is NOT an orthogonal cut basis:
    - y | Z, X \sim N(W_0 \eta_0 + W_1 \eta_1, \sigma^2 I)
    - under mild conditions, $\hat{\eta}$ converges $\eta^\star$, the projection of the true $E_\mathcal{F}(y | Z, X)$ on $W$
      - $$\eta^\star = \mathop{\arg \min}\limits_{\eta} E_\mathcal{F} \left[ (y - W \eta)^T (y - W\eta) | Z, X \right] = (W^T W)^{-1} W^T E_\mathcal{F}(y | Z, X)$$
    
    - entries in $\eta_1^\star$ associated with $z_i$ where $f_1(x, z) = 0$ can be non-zero.
    - False positives: frequentist & Bayesian tests give $\beta_j(z) \neq 0$ with probability 1 as $n \rightarrow \infty$.

  - ORTHOGONAL CUT BASIS:
    Split $z$ into regions (assume given for now).  An orthogonal cut basis is a basis for $\beta_{1k}(z)$ that has support only in a particular region (**cut**) and is **orthogonal** to bases outside that region.
    - in other words, entries in $\eta_1^\star$ are associated to only 1 region
    - columns in $W$ coding for group effects are orthogonal across regions
